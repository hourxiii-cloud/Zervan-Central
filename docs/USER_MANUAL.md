## 3. Understand the Room

The Room is the primary analytical object.

The operating model is one analytical object with multiple governed
representations.

A change in perspective does not by itself create another Room.

A Zone does not by itself create another Room.

A Space does not by itself create another Room.

A representation transform does not by itself create another Room.

Operate by rotating or narrowing the existing object when the object identity
has not changed.

Create distinct object identity only when the distinct-object rules support it.

---

## 5. Preserve Identity

Origin records where the object came from.

Genesis establishes governed object identity.

Revision changes the governed definition of the same object.

Branch preserves alternate lineage without silently declaring a distinct
analytical object.

Content hashes support integrity and identity.

A hash is not analytical truth.

A representation is not object identity.

A report is not the Room.

---

## 6. Qualify Before Analysis

Normal analysis follows qualification.

Qualification determines whether the requested operation has sufficient
assembly, provenance, boundaries, relationships, and permitted scope.

A ready request does not automatically mean the Room is ready.

A qualified Room does not automatically mean every operation is permitted.

When qualification is incomplete, the correct result may be provisional,
degraded, blocked, or rejected rather than fabricated certainty.

---

## 7. Occupancy

Occupancy represents governed presence in a Room.

Entry does not create ownership.

Presence does not create authority.

Exit does not erase the Room.

Occupancy state and analytical state remain distinct.

---

## 8. Ask a Question

Recurring inquiries use:

`contracts/question/QUESTION_CONTRACT.md`

A Question Contract records the inquiry in a reproducible form.

It carries the governed question together with scope, time, evidence boundary,
evidence ceiling, exclusions, required artifacts, audience, rendering
requirements, provenance, and Human Gate requirements.

A new question does not automatically create a new Room.

Version the Question Contract when its material semantics change.

---

## 9. Read-Only Inquiry

Question execution is read-only.

The Question Contract does not grant itself permission to write analytical
state.

If a requested operation would write, mutate, publish, populate, or execute
externally, that action requires its own governed authorization path.

Human and machine execution of the same Question Contract must preserve the
same inquiry semantics.

---

## 10. Evidence Boundary and Evidence Ceiling

The evidence boundary controls what evidence may legitimately participate in an
operation.

The evidence ceiling limits the strength of claims supported by that evidence.

Changing audience does not raise the ceiling.

Changing rendering does not raise the ceiling.

Personalization does not raise the ceiling.

Asking a broader question does not automatically broaden the boundary.

When evidence is insufficient, preserve the limit.

---

## 11. Route Capability Proportionally

Capability routing follows operational need.

Use the smallest justified capability that can answer the question.

Escalate when evidence justifies escalation.

Do not escalate merely because greater capability exists.

Scale and formation are separate concerns.

If mission conditions change enough to change formation, preserve the reason
for that change.

---

## 12. Hydrate on Need

Hydration releases what the mission requires.

Identity can travel while payload remains at rest.

Availability of data does not create standing permission to expose all of it.

Hydration remains bounded by the mission, Question Contract, evidence boundary,
evidence ceiling, restrictions, and current authorization.

---

## 13. Restriction and Constriction

Restriction limits what may be seen or used.

Constriction narrows the operational working surface.

Neither means the underlying material ceased to exist.

Do not silently convert restricted or excluded material into nonexistence.

Narrowing a view does not change object identity.

---

## 14. Perspective and Representation

When a different analytical perspective is required, change the representation
while preserving the object.

Preserve:

- Room identity;
- revision;
- lineage;
- provenance;
- active Zone;
- active Space;
- transform history;
- Cartography;
- evidence boundary;
- evidence ceiling.

Do not clone the analytical world merely to obtain another view.

---

## 15. Distinct Objects and Passageways

Different views of the same Room are not distinct objects.

A distinct object requires affirmative identity evidence.

When identity cannot yet be resolved, preserve that unresolved state.

Passageways connect genuinely distinct Rooms while preserving their
independence.

Relationship does not imply identity collapse.

---

## 16. Collapse

Collapse is a governed analytical condition produced by the evidence and active
constraints.

Collapse is not authority.

Collapse is not publication.

Collapse is not a new Room.

Preserve the evidence and route that produced the collapse state.

---

## 17. Landing

Landing preserves where an analytical operation arrived.

A useful Landing retains enough state to recover:

- Room identity;
- revision;
- question;
- current orientation;
- evidence boundary;
- evidence ceiling;
- unresolved conditions;
- relevant rendering state;
- provenance.

Landing is not Replay.

---

## 18. Reflight

Reflight resumes analytical movement when a governed trigger justifies doing
so.

Examples include a materially changed question, new evidence, or a changed
analytical requirement.

Reflight is not automatic repetition.

Preserve the reason for reflight.

---

## 19. Replay

Replay returns to the same Room at a historical observational coordinate.

Replay should preserve the historical conditions required to reproduce that
view.

Do not silently replace historical values with current defaults.

Replay does not create a new Room.

Replay does not recreate Genesis.

---

## 20. Scar and Scar Replay

A Scar preserves a meaningful unresolved or failure condition.

Do not erase a Scar merely because a later run succeeds.

Scar Replay permits inspection of that preserved condition in its attributable
historical context.

A reproducible Scar does not become analytical truth simply because it can be
replayed.

---

## 21. Analytical Pipeline

The native analytical route is:

Evidence
->
PMC
->
CCR
->
MC
->
Raven
->
Human Gate

PMC performs bounded analytical computation.

CCR preserves candidate commitment and lineage.

MC governs response and output admissibility.

Raven renders and reports.

Human Gate carries the authority-bearing decision boundary.

No stage inherits authority merely by receiving output from the prior stage.

---

## 22. Reports and Renderings

Reports are representations of governed Room state.

A report does not replace the Room.

A rendering may change presentation for audience or format.

Rendering must preserve the governed analytical meaning and evidence limits.

Trace report claims back to their evidence, question, boundaries, and
provenance.

---

## 23. Decision Support

Zervan may prepare decision options.

A decision option is not itself the decision.

Decision options must remain traceable to analytical and evidence lineage.

Human Gate determines whether an authority-bearing decision is made.

Approval is not automatic execution.

---

## 24. Blocked, Provisional, Degraded, and Rejected Operation

A blocked result is valid when a required condition is missing.

A provisional result may preserve bounded useful state while qualification or
evidence remains incomplete.

A degraded result should preserve both what remains usable and what has been
lost or restricted.

A rejected route remains part of provenance.

Do not guess simply to avoid a blocked or unresolved result.

---

## 25. Uncertainty

Uncertainty is preserved rather than hidden.

When available evidence cannot support a winner, null or unresolved outcomes are
valid.

Unknown does not automatically become true.

Unknown does not automatically become false.

Claim strength remains bounded by the evidence ceiling.

---

## 26. Failure Handling

When behavior fails validation:

1. preserve the observed failure;
2. identify the affected surface;
3. classify the failure;
4. repair the smallest correct surface;
5. rerun bounded validation;
6. preserve the repair provenance.

Ring 6 carries these failure classes:

- `INNER_INVARIANT_CONTRADICTION`
- `OUTER_IMPLEMENTATION_DEFECT`
- `VALIDATOR_DEFECT`
- `COVERAGE_GAP`
- `SOURCE_COLLISION`
- `UNRESOLVED_REQUIRED_SURFACE`

Do not change implementation merely to satisfy an incorrect validator.

Do not weaken doctrine merely to obtain green output.

---

## 27. Returning to Existing Work

When returning to a Room, recover existing state before creating new state.

Recover at least:

- object identity;
- revision;
- prior question;
- current or prior Landing;
- orientation;
- Cartography;
- evidence boundary;
- evidence ceiling;
- restrictions;
- unresolved conditions.

Then determine whether to continue, rotate, reflight, replay, branch, or remain
closed.

---

## 28. Normal User Operating Sequence

A normal analytical interaction follows this general sequence:

1. Resolve native version and repository entry.
2. Identify the Room.
3. Recover or establish orientation.
4. State the question.
5. Establish the Question Contract where required.
6. Confirm scope and time window.
7. Confirm evidence boundary.
8. Confirm evidence ceiling.
9. Confirm qualification and readiness.
10. Select proportionate capability.
11. Hydrate only mission-required material.
12. Analyze within the governed object.
13. Preserve transforms, Cartography, exclusions, and uncertainty.
14. Collapse only when supported.
15. Land with continuity intact.
16. Render through the reporting path.
17. Prepare decision options when required.
18. Pass authority-bearing decisions to Human Gate.
19. Perform no external action without separate authorization.
20. Preserve enough state for return, Replay, and audit.
